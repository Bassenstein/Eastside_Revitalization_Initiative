01

Executive summary

Illustrated here using Ward 8 as an example, this plan is a targeted approach to remediating hazardous, blighted, and destabilizing vacant parcels — in this case, within the Hough residential interior.

This proposal requests $1,095,000 in Community Development Block Grant funding to stabilize approximately 120–150 vacant lots through forestry management, debris removal, soil remediation, grading, erosion control, turf establishment, and perimeter safety improvements. The request comprises the $1,000,000 base scope plus a $95,000 forestry management addendum (§3A). These activities will reduce neighborhood blight, improve safety, support future development, and enhance quality of life for low- and moderate-income residents.

02

Statement of need

Ward 8's Hough interior contains one of the highest concentrations of vacant parcels in Cleveland. These lots contribute to illegal dumping, rodent harborage, erosion, concealment for criminal activity, depressed property values, and unsafe walking conditions.

More than 85% of residents in the project area are low- or moderate-income, meeting HUD's LMI Area Benefit criteria. The vacant lots are officially classified as blight, qualifying the project under Slum/Blight Elimination.

Framing discipline

This is not beautification. HUD will not fund nice lawns; HUD funds site stabilization, hazard mitigation, and blight removal that happens to include turf restoration. Every activity in this proposal is written, budgeted, and reported as blight elimination — the language must hold from application through closeout.

Scope of work

  • Forestry management — hazard tree removal, pruning, brush and invasive clearing, stump grinding. Sequenced ahead of ground work; see §3A.
  • Debris removal and blight clearance — dumped material, tires, construction waste, overgrowth.
  • Soil remediation and regrading — correcting grade failure, drainage, and unsafe depressions.
  • Erosion control and hydroseeding — stabilizing exposed soil against runoff and re-dumping.
  • Turf establishment — durable ground cover that makes lots maintainable and visibly claimed.
  • Perimeter safety — fencing, bollards, and edge treatment where lots front active pedestrian routes.
  • Community-use preparation — readying strategic parcels for infill housing, gardens, and pocket parks.

All activities are fully eligible under CDBG regulations.

03A

Forestry management — addendum

Unkept, hazardous, and overgrown trees on blighted vacant lots are themselves a blighting influence, and on many parcels they are the condition that blocks every other activity in this scope.

A lot cannot be regraded, seeded, or fenced with a dead ash standing over it. This addendum adds forestry management as an eligible and necessary component of site remediation, sequenced ahead of debris removal and grading rather than alongside it.

Why unmaintained trees qualify as blight

  • Falling-limb hazards over sidewalks, adjacent property, and pedestrian routes.
  • Obstructed sight lines for pedestrians and vehicles.
  • Concealment for illegal dumping and criminal activity.
  • Sidewalk and curb damage through root intrusion.
  • Fire hazard from dead or dry vegetation.

These conditions meet HUD's definition of blighting influences and qualify for remediation under 24 CFR 570.201(c) (public facilities and improvements), 24 CFR 570.208(b) (slum/blight elimination, area basis), and HUD blight criteria for unsafe, deteriorated, or hazardous site conditions.

Technical specifications

SPEC A

Tree removal

Remove dead, diseased, or hazardous trees. Certified arborists on all high-risk removals. Safe drop zones and traffic control established before work begins.

SPEC B

Pruning & trimming

ANSI A300 pruning standards. Remove low-hanging limbs obstructing sidewalks or streets; clear canopy to restore visibility and safety.

SPEC C

Brush clearing

Remove overgrown vegetation and invasive species, including tree-of-heaven. Chip or dispose of brush at licensed facilities.

SPEC D

Stump grinding

Grind stumps to 6–10 inches below grade and backfill with screened topsoil, leaving the parcel ready for regrading.

SPEC E

Root hazard mitigation

Address root systems damaging sidewalks or curbs, coordinated with sidewalk remediation teams so the pavement fix follows the root fix.

SPEC F

Final restoration

Hydroseed disturbed areas and conduct a closing safety inspection for remaining hazards before the parcel is signed off.

Scope check — inventory before award

At $95,000 the forestry line averages roughly $700 across all 135 parcels, which is well under the cost of a single hazard-tree removal. Read correctly, this line funds a subset of parcels — on the order of 50–70 hazard-tree removals with stump grinding, plus brush clearing across a wider set. A tree inventory and hazard assessment in Phase 1 should establish that subset before contracts are let, so the line item is defended by a parcel count rather than an average.

The forestry line covers labor, equipment, disposal, and safety measures. Direct site work now totals $895,000 (81.7%), up from $800,000 (80.0%) at REV A. Program administration at 13.7% of the request sits below HUD's 20% planning and administration ceiling. At 135 parcels the blended cost is ~$8,100 per lot; across the 120–150 range, $7,300–$9,100.

If the ask must hold at $1,000,000

Forestry is treated here as added scope, not absorbed scope, because it is a precondition for the ground work rather than a substitute for it. If the city requires a flat $1.0M ceiling, the two clean levers are: (1) hold the budget and reduce the parcel count to roughly 120–125 lots, or (2) hold the parcel count and defer community-use preparation ($75,000) plus $20,000 of perimeter safety to a follow-on year. Do not absorb forestry by trimming soil remediation — that is the line that determines whether a stabilized lot stays stabilized.

09

CDBG compliance justification

This project meets HUD's national objectives on two independent grounds, and every proposed activity maps to a cited eligible-activity provision.

NATIONAL OBJECTIVE — 24 CFR 570.208(b)

Slum/blight elimination, area basis

All parcels are officially classified as blight due to dumping, erosion, overgrowth, unsafe conditions, and deteriorated site conditions. Cleveland's own planning documents categorize vacant lots — especially Land Bank parcels — as blighting influences.

NATIONAL OBJECTIVE — LMI AREA BENEFIT

Low- and moderate-income area benefit

The project area is majority LMI at more than 85%, exceeding HUD's threshold. Benefit accrues to the area as a whole rather than to individual beneficiaries, which fits a lot-based scope.

ELIGIBLE ACTIVITIES — 570.201(c), 570.202

Clearance and site improvement

Forestry management, debris removal, remediation, grading, erosion control, turf establishment, and perimeter safety all fall within public facilities and site improvements and rehabilitation provisions.

PRECEDENT — CITY PRACTICE

Consistent with existing city spending

Cleveland already applies CDBG to vacant-lot stabilization, post-demolition site restoration, LMI neighborhood revitalization, and Land Bank property preparation. This extends established practice to standing vacant lots.

Why vacant lots qualify as blight

  • No active use, and often overgrown or poorly maintained.
  • Contributes to neighborhood deterioration and depresses surrounding property values.
  • Creates safety issues — dumping, concealment, pests.
  • Presents environmental hazards and unsafe or unhealthy conditions.
  • Carries unmaintained or dead vegetation — falling limbs, blocked sight lines, root damage to public sidewalk and curb.
10

Evaluation & reporting

Success will be measured by the number of lots stabilized, reduction in dumping complaints, reduction in erosion hazards, improved parcel conditions documented via GIS, resident satisfaction surveys, and before/after photographic documentation. Quarterly reports will be submitted to Cleveland's Department of Community Development and HUD.

Conclusion

As this Ward 8 example shows, the Ward Vacant Lot Stabilization Plan is a high-impact, CDBG-eligible approach that would significantly improve safety, stability, and livability in the neighborhoods it serves.

This proposal provides a clear, compliant, and actionable plan to eliminate blight, support future development, and enhance quality of life for low- and moderate-income residents.
