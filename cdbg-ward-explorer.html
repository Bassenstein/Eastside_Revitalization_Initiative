The plan — same structure in every ward

Every ward would use the same plan. Only the inputs change: which parcels, how many, and which priority zones. Ward 8 is used as an example throughout; the full Ward 8 example is in the Ward Vacant Lot Stabilization Plan.

Scope of work

  • Forestry management — hazard-tree removal, pruning, brush and invasive clearing, stump grinding; sequenced ahead of ground work.
  • Debris removal & blight clearance — dumped material, tires, construction waste, overgrowth.
  • Soil remediation & regrading — correcting grade failure, drainage, and unsafe depressions.
  • Erosion control & hydroseeding — stabilizing exposed soil against runoff and re-dumping.
  • Turf establishment — durable ground cover that makes lots maintainable and visibly cared for.
  • Perimeter safety — fencing, bollards, and edge treatment where lots front active pedestrian routes.
  • Community-use preparation — readying strategic parcels for infill housing, gardens, and pocket parks.
Framing discipline

This is not beautification. CDBG funds site stabilization, hazard mitigation, and blight removal that happens to include turf restoration. Recurring mowing and maintenance stay a City cost — CDBG pays for the one-time improvement only.

CDBG eligibility — how a ward qualifies

24 CFR 570.208(a)(1)

Low/moderate-income area benefit

An activity qualifies when its service area is at least 51% low- and moderate-income (or the City's HUD-approved exception threshold). The ward profile above shows how many block groups clear 51%; a submission must document the actual service area with HUD LMISD data.

24 CFR 570.208(b)(1)

Slum/blight — area basis

The City must designate the area's boundaries under a State or local blight definition, show a substantial share of properties (HUD applies a 25% test) meet defined conditions, and re-determine the designation at least every 10 years.

24 CFR 570.201(c) & (d)

Eligible activities

Clearance and debris removal, plus public-facility and site improvements — forestry hazard work, grading, erosion control, and perimeter safety tied to documented conditions.

Guardrails

What CDBG won't pay for

Recurring maintenance and routine mowing, general government operations, and anything that hasn't cleared environmental review (24 CFR Part 58) before funds are committed.

06

What any ward needs before a submission

  • A scored parcel list. Phase 1 parcel scoring and GIS mapping, starting from the ward's City Land Bank inventory shown above.
  • Low/mod-income documentation. The service area's LMI percentage from HUD LMISD block-group data.
  • A slum/blight area designation. Boundaries, the local definition relied on, and a condition survey meeting HUD's threshold.
  • Environmental review. Completed under 24 CFR Part 58 before any funds are committed.
  • Ward priorities. Zones, tiers, and sequencing shaped with residents and the ward's Council office.

Sources & method

  • Ward boundaries & Council roster — City of Cleveland Open Data, 2026 wards (Ord. No. 1-2025), including each ward's published Council member and office contact.
  • People & housing — U.S. Census Bureau American Community Survey 5-year estimates by ward, as published by the City of Cleveland (latest vintage shown). ACS estimates carry margins of error.
  • Low/moderate income — HUD Low/Mod Income Summary Data by block group, as published on City of Cleveland Open Data. Each block group is assigned to the ward containing its center.
  • City Land Bank lots — City of Cleveland Land Bank lots layer, counted live within each ward boundary. A lot crossing a ward line can count in both wards.
  • Neighborhoods — City of Cleveland Statistical Planning Area (SPA) neighborhoods; each neighborhood's share of a ward's area is estimated by point sampling and rounded.
  • Estimator — scaled line-by-line from the Ward 8 example budget (REV C: $1,095,000 for 135 parcels). It is a modeling aid, not a cost estimate for any specific ward.

All figures are planning-level context, not eligibility determinations. Data are read live from public services each time the page loads, so numbers can change as the City updates them.
